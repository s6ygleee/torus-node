Framework and claim boundaries

From torus data to a conditional physical model

The program studies a finite construction built from flat two-torus nodes. Its continuum and physical interpretation is a research target, not an established consequence of the microscopic source.

Reading rule. “Established” below means established within the declared finite mathematical model or its released formal cores. It does not mean experimentally validated, peer reviewed, or derived from an unrestricted continuum source.
DefinitionModel input

Discrete torus-node geometry

A node is specified by area (or logarithmic area), a modulus τ in the upper half-plane, rotor phase, internal state, and winding data. The flat torus is

T²τ = ℂ / (ℤ + τℤ),   A = A₀eρ.

Modularly equivalent values of τ represent the same torus shape. The manuscript takes a finite bounded-geometry completion as declared model data. Deriving that completion uniquely from an unrestricted random source remains open.

Finite constructionContinuum stability open

D4 spatial moves

The finite model uses a D4 root/move system to organize discrete directions and dispersion data. Within the declared construction, this supports a four-dimensional carrier and a distinguished temporal/spatial split. Whether the selection is stable under source perturbations, coarse-graining, and continuum scaling is an open problem.

Finite arithmetic

Level-3 commensurability

A level-three cusp sector and determinant-two Hecke correspondences supply a finite permutation structure. In the paper’s construction, these extend an A4 cusp action to an S4 action. This arithmetic sector and the D4 source are separately specified; one should not be presented as deriving the other without an additional bridge.

Algebraic coreRepresentation choice

S4 ordered-history algebra

Rather than using only the four-cusp permutation representation, the construction retains an algebra of ordered spatial-move histories. Its right-regular commutant and selected matter-coupled blocks are used to identify finite gauge factors. The choice of retained blocks and their physical interpretation must remain visible when stating the result.

Conditional reconstructionSource bridge open

Emergent spacetime

The finite model proposes four kinematic directions and an oriented temporal direction. A Lorentzian theory requires more: a common interacting continuum family, reflection positivity, Euclidean covariance, regularity, clustering, and a valid Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction. Those model-specific requirements are not all proved.

finite Euclidean model + OS premises ⟹ conditional Lorentzian reconstruction
Block-dependent

Gauge structure

The ordered-history commutant supplies finite unitary factors. After specified central combinations are removed and charged matter blocks are retained, the manuscript identifies the familiar SU(3)c × SU(2)L × U(1)Y form. This is a structural result inside the finite conditional model; the physical continuum gauge theory and Wilson/source universality bridges remain conditional.

Declared modulePhenomenology incomplete

Chiral matter

A declared finite matter module is used to study chirality, charge quantization, and anomaly sums. The release contains formal and certified finite components, but does not derive the full matter sector uniquely from unrestricted node data. Quark masses, CKM and PMNS mixing, and neutrino masses are not reproduced at leading order in the current account.

Dynamical-metric axiomDerivative expansion

Effective gravitational dynamics

The dynamical-metric branch adds an independent metric axiom: metrics with fixed volume form are integrated with a chosen effective weight. The manuscript then seeks a two-derivative Einstein–Hilbert form. The metric axiom is not derived from the minimal node axioms, and the low-energy derivative expansion and coefficient identification are additional conditions.

Γ[g] = ∫ d⁴x √g (Λ + cRR + higher derivatives)
Open interface

Poisson-source interface

The ambitious source reading begins with an unrestricted Poisson distribution of torus nodes. The current record does not show that this raw source uniquely selects the relation skeleton, transport, state–field map, action coefficients, or an exact source-identical renormalization map. Different compatible transports can change nonlinear data.

Primary research boundary

Continuum reconstruction

The paper states a conditional source-to-continuum theorem with eight classes of premises: a microscopic source; uniform stability and moments; physical reflection positivity; a same-measure RG map; retained sectors or a controlled complement; Ward identities with controlled remainders; continuum tightness/equicontinuity; and pole/vertex identification. The unrestricted source has not been shown to satisfy this package.
