Formal methods and reproducibility

What has been checked—and what has not

The Lean archive makes finite statements inspectable and replayable. It is evidence about a formal model, not a substitute for semantic review, continuum analysis, or empirical validation.

Verification layers

Machine checked

Finite formal cores

Lean declarations, pinned dependencies, placeholder checks, dependency closure, and statement mappings represented in the released tree.

Needs semantic judgment

Paper-to-code correspondence

A syntactic mapping does not by itself prove that human-language and formal statements have identical meaning or physical scope.

Not supplied by a build

Model-specific analysis

Tightness of the interacting family, full OS reconstruction, source selection, cutoff removal, and universality bridges.

Empirical question

Physical validation

Agreement with selected measurements does not establish unique microscopic derivation or validate untested predictions.

Reproduce the public archive

Clone the separate Lean repository and use its pinned toolchain. The ordinary entry point builds with one job and runs bounded audits; numerical certificates have their own recorded inputs and commands.

git clone https://github.com/s6ygleee/torus-node-lean4.git
cd torus-node-lean4
lake build -K jobs=1
./verify_all.sh

Consult the repository’s README and certificate-specific instructions before interpreting a pass. The full audit can be memory intensive, and optional independent replay may be skipped when its separate toolchain is unavailable.

Independently verifiable targets

  • Rebuild Lean from the pinned manifest and inspect the axiom output.
  • Compare each manuscript claim with its formal declaration and hypotheses.
  • Replay exact and interval-certified calculations from declared inputs.
  • Challenge a boundary statement with a proof, counterexample, or missing-premise report.
  • Verify that no continuum or physical conclusion is inferred solely from a finite core.
