Independent theoretical research

About the initiative

The Torus-Node Research Initiative is a public framework for developing, checking, and challenging a proposed model of emergent spacetime, gravity, gauge structure, and matter.

Purpose

The initiative exists to make the research record easier to inspect: the mathematical construction, its formal verification resources, the assumptions that carry physical interpretations, and the open problems that require new work.

It is not presented as a university center, company, collaboration, or endorsed institute. In the absence of verified institutional information, it should be understood as an independent theoretical research initiative.

Initiating researcher

Youngik (Tom) Lee initiated the Torus-Node research program and is the author of the current preprint and associated public Lean 4 archive.

No university appointment, institutional partnership, funding relationship, or external endorsement is asserted on this site.

Research posture

The project treats a model as useful when its claims can be separated into precise layers: definition, finite theorem, conditional inference, computational result, phenomenological comparison, conjecture, and open problem. The portal is designed to keep those layers visible.

No claim of completion. The program does not claim that the Standard Model, quantum gravity, a grand unified theory, or a “theory of everything” has been experimentally established or fully proved.

Operating principles

Open work, bounded claims

Traceability

Link public claims to a manuscript section, declaration, certificate, or clearly marked research question.

Falsifiability

Publish assumptions and unresolved interfaces so counterexamples can target the real claim.

Reproducibility

Preserve code, dependency locks, inputs, outputs, and versioned citations.
