Participation protocol

Contribute a checkable piece of research

Contributions are evaluated on technical merit, clarity of assumptions, attribution, and reproducibility—not on whether they support the model.

Start with an IssueOpen a Discussion

Reproduce a mathematical result

Identify the exact paper version, theorem or certificate, toolchain, and commit. Record every command and any deviation from the pinned environment. Report passes and failures with logs that do not contain secrets or machine-private paths.

Report a possible error

Use the “Counterexample or possible error” template. Quote the statement, explain the minimal conflict, and state whether the issue is formal, semantic, numerical, bibliographic, or physical. A concise failing example is ideal.

Submit a counterexample

Provide complete definitions, verify that the example satisfies the original hypotheses, and show the exact conclusion that fails. Include code or data when computation is involved.

Propose a theorem

State hypotheses and conclusion independently of the desired physical interpretation. Explain which open dependency it resolves and whether the claim is finite, asymptotic, continuum, or empirical.

Contribute Lean 4 code

Work against the separate Lean repository. Keep declarations small, avoid unapproved axioms and placeholders, include tests or audit updates, and connect prose claims to exact declarations.

Submit an independent calculation

Include source code, immutable inputs, software versions, expected output, uncertainty treatment, and a short statement of what the calculation does—and does not—show.

Propose collaboration

Use a Research Proposal Discussion. Name the question, method, required expertise, ownership and attribution expectations, milestones, and a criterion for success or falsification.

Five things every technical contribution needs

Statement

The exact claim being addressed.

Assumptions

All logical and computational inputs.

Method

A reviewable derivation or procedure.

Evidence

Proof, code, data, or counterexample.

Scope

What follows—and what does not.
